## 4. Revised visual system

### Project fields

Replace solid platforms with irregular, fine-grained fields. Many dim particles and a small number of emphasized strands create depth. Stable project labels remain the main landmarks. Most field detail is decorative texture, not a hidden inventory of actual files, agents or links.

Project hue identifies geography. Actor identity comes from the crawler's mark, label and selected inspector. Attention and source loss use separate text/state treatment. Color alone must not carry a factual distinction.

### Original crawlers

Separate four layers:

1. Short articulated limbs: locomotion and character motion only.
2. Long probes: observed current resource interactions.
3. Body movement: a change in the execution's current work context.
4. Contact response: a small local acknowledgement or signal.

The full-detail selected crawler uses richer articulation; nearby helpers have simpler bodies. A role emblem does not substitute for a session identity. The default scene must show several asynchronously active teams so the design proves more than a single character showcase.

Keep helpers spatially distinct. A leader with two workers should read as three bodies, not as one creature with additional limbs. Place them at their respective resource targets and reveal qualified parent edges on selection.

### Motion grammar

Proposed production starting ranges:

| Motion | Starting range | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local body move | 180–360 ms | Immediate change of work focus |
| Cross-region move | 450–850 ms | Readable transfer of attention/context |
| Arrival settlement | 80–140 ms | Weight and continuity |
| Probe extension | 120–220 ms | Prompt response to an observed target |
| Contact/retraction | Tuned to span duration and readability | A contact does not invent another tool call |
| Camera focus | 450–700 ms | Deliberate user-controlled orientation |
| Short caption | Usually 1–2 seconds when useful | Readable explanation; recent activity labeled as recent |

These are design ranges, not measurements from the original video. The study's base-clock travel uses 310 ms locally and 720 ms for longer jumps; its 1.5× default is explicitly scenario playback. Production Live time remains actual observed time. Faster choreography must not speed up completion, reorder facts or misstate real latency.

The camera is stable by default. Follow is optional and uses a safe region. User manipulation takes control. No every-event auto-pan, random walking to imply productivity, or indefinitely growing animation queue.

### People-first interface

The video supplies a visual vocabulary, not the default information hierarchy. Use a few stable questions: what objective, which team, what activity, what blocker, what actual outcome. Exact command/path/branch/receipt detail is selectable.

Word-consumption gauges and synthetic quality scores are not appropriate substitutions for operational evidence. Live token metrics can be added only where their provider/source actually reports the relevant quantity and units.

Previously confirmed terminal results stay recorded rather than being erased by subsequent feed loss. Restoration reconciles the current snapshot without replaying an obsolete backlog.

## 7. Runtime architecture and performance

The live pipeline remains:

~~~text
existing qualified owners
  → incumbent Living HQ / estate adapter
  → source-qualified snapshot and trace deltas
  → stable layout + bounded choreography
  → one renderer + existing HTML inspector/navigation
~~~

The study's geometry and fixture are not a new telemetry contract. Production adaptation performs no network IO, polling, dispatch, persistence or lifecycle ownership. Streams/cursors and bounded polling stay with their existing owners.

### The important optimization

Most of the visually dense world should be static geometry or cached texture. Animate a small active subset: creatures, current probes, travel impulses and selected highlights. A field containing thousands of decorative points is not thousands of React components or every-frame network operations.

The study caches its six fields, caps DPR at 1.5, updates HTML at a lower cadence than rendering, suspends display scheduling while hidden, and disposes listeners, timers, resize observation and textures. Its textures are currently baked synchronously at construction; production must measure and move that work off the interaction-critical path or use prebaked assets.

PixiJS remains the production preference for many visible actors: batched sprites/meshes, cached geometry, bounded labels and a measured quality ladder. Its [performance guidance](https://pixijs.com/8.x/guides/concepts/performance-tips) supports avoiding constantly rebuilt complex graphics and large numbers of filters. Full 3D remains an optional comparative experiment.

### Revised visual-detail budget

The reference's rich appendages warrant a stricter readability budget than the original proposal's maximum full-rig count:

- Approximately **6–8 richly articulated actors** in the viewport.
- Approximately **20–30 additional simplified actors**, with larger populations grouped.
- At most roughly three strongly emphasized stories at once.
- Approximately six nearby resource labels for the selected actor.
- Stable grouping over qualified logical snapshots; source omissions stay separate from visual aggregation.

Keep the original 1,000-summary / 30-project stress fixture and 100 events/s sustained / 1,000 events/s burst proposal. These remain proposed qualification loads, not proven throughput. Preserve actual source/decoder limits; decorative particle counts do not count toward semantic entity coverage.

Targets remain 60 Hz where qualified, a stable 30 Hz lower tier, main-thread animation bookkeeping p95 below 2 ms, shell interaction p95 below 100 ms without material baseline regression, hidden-view suspension, bounded memory and repeated-mount disposal. Measure exact devices and browsers. No universal FPS guarantee is inferred from the uploaded 60 fps video or native raster checks.
