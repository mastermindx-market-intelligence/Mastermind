## Regulatory and first-party sources

<a id="r01"></a>
### R01 — SEC Form 13F FAQ

[SEC Form 13F frequently asked questions](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/frequently-asked-questions-about-form-13f).

Primary staff guidance inspected for deadline, reportable universe, units, options, de-minimis omissions, amendments/confidential treatment and the Q4 2025 official-list TXT change. Relevant questions include 10, 35–44 and 58. Guidance explains the form but does not replace applicable rule/form text. Used to reject whole-portfolio, confirmed-exit and unit-homogeneity assumptions.

[Official 13F datasets](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-13f-data-sets).

[Official rulemaking page](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2026/02/s7-2026-05); [IC-35962 proposal PDF](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/proposed/2026/ic-35962.pdf).

No later finalization was established in this bounded official-source check.

## Primary academic literature

<a id="p01"></a>
### P01 — Mutual-fund herding

Russ Wermers, 1999, [Mutual Fund Herding and the Impact on Stock Prices](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0022-1082.00118). Historical ownership/return evidence; not a current filing-latency-safe strategy proof.

<a id="p02"></a>
### P02 — Institutional herding and feedback trading

Nofsinger and Sias, 1999, [Herding and Feedback Trading by Institutional and Individual Investors](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0022-1082.00188). Supports competing momentum/contemporaneous-price explanations.

<a id="p03"></a>
### P03 — Flow-induced fire sales

Coval and Stafford, [Asset Fire Sales (and Purchases) in Equity Markets](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11357), NBER working paper 11357. A mechanism for non-informational price pressure; no present-day effect size imported.

<a id="p04"></a>
### P04 — Insider transaction heterogeneity

Cohen, Malloy and Pomorski, [Decoding Inside Information](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16454), NBER working paper 16454. Motivates prior-only routine/opportunistic classifications; no full-sample label leakage permitted.

<a id="p05"></a>
### P05 — Activism and real outcomes

Brav, Jiang and Kim, [The Real Effects of Hedge Fund Activism](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17517), NBER working paper 17517. Economic mechanism research, not randomized proof of a post-filing trading rule.

<a id="p06"></a>
### P06 — Longer-run activism

Bebchuk, Brav and Jiang, [The Long-Term Effects of Hedge Fund Activism](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21227), NBER working paper 21227. Longer-horizon outcomes and competing interpretations must remain distinct from immediately executable returns.

<a id="p07"></a>
### P07 — Blockholder mechanisms

Edmans, [Blockholders and Corporate Governance](https://www.nber.org/papers/w19573), NBER working paper 19573. Voice/exit and associated tradeoffs; does not justify a uniformly positive concentration score.

<a id="p08"></a>
### P08 — ETF ownership and volatility

Ben-David, Franzoni and Moussawi, [Do ETFs Increase Volatility?](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20071), NBER working paper 20071. Supports a separately tested volatility/liquidity channel, not an ownership-direction alpha claim.

## What was not established

Native source acquisition, inspected contract/PR evidence, mathematical reasoning and regulatory/vendor literature are deliberately separate evidence classes. Unknowns and action-specific refusals are recorded in the final report and audit register rather than replaced with assumed completeness.
