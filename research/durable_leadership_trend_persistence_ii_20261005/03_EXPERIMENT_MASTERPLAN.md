# 03 — Successor Strategy Comparison and Experiment Masterplan

## Strategy comparison

### Strategy A — finer static group persistence

**Question:** Do industry-group / industry / honest fixed-basket states predict continuation and leader durability?

**Strengths:** Strong external mechanism prior; interpretable; easier to freeze than dynamic themes; compatible with leave-issuer-out peer evidence.

**Risks:** Historical PIT taxonomy is the main data gate; common-factor momentum can masquerade as group confirmation; overly narrow groups become sparse.

### Strategy B — dynamic theme persistence

**Question:** Does canonical point-in-time theme membership plus ThemeState, breadth, continuity, entropy, and later flow/fundamental evidence predict future theme or constituent durability?

**Strengths:** Highest product fidelity for emerging themes and narrative leadership; naturally supports breadth, continuity, and leader-dependency questions.

**Risks:** Serious PIT-membership and hindsight risk; limited honest historical depth; must consume GMI Theme Graph rather than invent a new taxonomy; likely prospective at first.

### Strategy C — direct leadership-survival / hazard model

**Question:** Forget “group feature predicts raw return” as the primary framing. Estimate whether a current leader remains a leader, how long it remains one, and when its break hazard rises.

**Strengths:** Best alignment with the product question; interpretable; naturally handles censoring; separates state durability from raw return; directly supports monitoring/holding decisions.

**Risks:** State definitions and horizons must be frozen; overlapping observations create dependence; survival skill does not automatically imply return alpha.

### Ranking

| Strategy | Scientific plausibility | Distinct from B2/C1 | PIT readiness | Fresh-evidence efficiency | Product fit | Ruling |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A — finer static groups | High | High if PIT and leave-one-out | Medium | High with licensed history; slow prospectively | High | **Second** |
| B — dynamic themes | High but less established | Very high | Low today; improving prospectively | Low initially | Very high | **Third / future replication** |
| C — direct survival/hazard | **Highest** | **Highest** | High once state definition is fixed | High | **Highest** | **First** |
| Hybrid C+A | **Highest** | **Highest** | Medium | **Highest credible near-term route** | **Highest** | **Recommended** |

**RULING:** Strategy C wins on endpoint; Strategy A supplies the first group substrate. Strategy B is the later prospective replication once GMI has accumulated enough uncontaminated PIT history.

## Bounded successor sequence

The sequence should destroy hypotheses faster than it creates features.

| Experiment | Core question | Evidence | Advance gate | Permanent closure on failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DL-0 — PIT qualification** | Can the grouping be reconstructed without survivorship/taxonomy leakage? | No predictive outcomes | Effective dates, identity, delistings, rights, coverage all pass | Dataset/group route only |
| **DL-1 — Independent-peer leadership survival** | Does finer-group peer state predict leader survival beyond own stock? | Independent ex-U.S. PIT history **or** prospective U.S. | Group-only validity + incremental stock+group material improvement | Frozen static industry-group continuity feature family for this target |
| **DL-2 — Stock × group / break hazard** | Is group state useful for selecting which leaders survive/fail, not merely timing groups? | New untouched countries/time block | Interaction/additive model beats stock-only and group-only; hazard calibrated | Stock-selection interpretation; retain group timing only if supported |
| **DL-3 — Dynamic-theme replication** | Does result generalize to PIT market themes? | Only post-freeze GMI vintages | Same directional/material relationship under canonical theme identity | Dynamic-theme generalization |

DL-0 is a data-admission gate rather than an efficacy experiment.

## DL-0 — PIT and rights qualification

### Hypothesis

The proposed group substrate can be constructed using information that was actually effective at each formation date, with sufficient identity, delisting, coverage, and lawful model-use rights to support an untouched test.

### Null

The substrate cannot be shown to be point-in-time, identity-safe, survivorship-safe, or lawfully usable.

### Required checks

- Historical classification records have explicit effective-from / effective-through semantics.
- Company identifiers allow focal-issuer and alternate-listing exclusion.
- Dead/delisted securities remain in the eligible historical universe where appropriate.
- Universe membership is point-in-time.
- Price adjustments and trading-session clocks are stable.
- The source license permits the intended research/model use and storage.
- Coverage is sufficient for the frozen peer-count floor at the primary grouping.
- No outcome-driven taxonomy repair is allowed.

### Advance gate

All required PIT, identity, rights, and coverage checks pass before outcome access.

### Kill gate

Any unresolved backcasting, survivorship, identity, rights, or effective-date defect that could change the group state.

### Closure

A DL-0 failure closes the **dataset/group route**, not the durable-leadership thesis. The next action is another predeclared admissible data route or WAIT FOR DATA — not a hidden backfill.

## DL-1 — Independent-peer leadership survival

### Core hypothesis

For a stock that is a current relative-strength leader, independent peer continuity and breadth within its point-in-time GICS industry group predict subsequent leader-state survival after controlling for rich own-stock momentum, residual momentum, beta, and volatility.

### Null

After those own-stock and common-factor controls, the out-of-sample predictive contribution of the frozen independent group feature family is zero or economically immaterial.

### Primary target

Top-quartile leader survival at 20 sessions.

### Secondary confirmatory target

Top-quartile leader survival at 60 sessions.

### Structural secondary target

Time to first leader-state exit, right-censored at 60 sessions.

### Material-break secondary target

First entry into the bottom half of the cross-sectional leadership rank during the 60-session window.

### Mandatory model views

- **N:** nuisance/base-rate model.
- **G:** group-only predictors + nuisance controls.
- **S:** strong stock-only baseline.
- **A:** S + G.
- **I:** S + G + frozen stock×group interactions.

Principal contrasts:

- G − N: does the group state contain real predictive information?
- A − S: does group evidence add to a strong stock baseline?
- I − A: does stock×group interaction improve selection?

### Interpretation gate

If G predicts but A does not beat S, the result is **group timing/context**, not stock-selection skill.

### Advance gate

DL-1 advances only if:

- A − S passes the prespecified 20-session statistical gate;
- the effect exceeds the frozen minimum effect worth caring about;
- G − N carries real predictive information;
- 60-session evidence is directionally consistent and passes if reached in the fixed sequence;
- calibration is acceptable;
- sign is stable across prespecified time blocks;
- no single country, group, or episode dominates;
- PIT/identity quality stays above the frozen floor;
- residual-momentum / common-factor challengers do not absorb the effect.

### Kill gate

Close the DL-1 static-group peer-confirmation hypothesis if:

- no material A − S improvement exists at 20 sessions;
- the apparent effect disappears against residual/common-factor momentum;
- G contains no independent predictive information;
- the effect exists only when the focal issuer contaminates its group;
- the result depends on current-taxonomy backcasting or future membership information;
- one group/country/episode explains the result;
- calibration is unusable despite ranking significance.

### Permanent closure

A failed DL-1 permanently closes this **frozen industry-group predictor family for this frozen leader-survival definition**. Do not rename peer_continuity or shift the horizon on the same evidence.

## DL-2 — Stock × group interaction and break hazard

Run only if DL-1 advances.

### Core question

Can group state distinguish:

- leader in a strong / broadening group;
- leader in a strong / narrowing group;
- secondary / catch-up stock in a strong group;
- leader in a weak group?

### Required fresh evidence

A new untouched country block or future-time block. DL-1 confirmation observations cannot adjudicate DL-2.

### Primary gate

The additive / interaction model must improve selection beyond both S and G, and the hazard model must remain calibrated.

### Kill interpretation

If G works but A/I do not, close the **stock-selection interpretation**. Preserve a narrower group-level timing/context interpretation only if it independently survives its own gates.

---

## DL-3 — Prospective dynamic-theme replication

Run only after static-group evidence survives.

### Substrate

Canonical GMI Theme Graph point-in-time membership and ThemeState only.

### Hypothesis

The conceptual predictors that survived DL-1/DL-2 — continuity, breadth, leader dependency, and group strength — generalize to evolving market themes.

### Fresh-evidence rule

Only post-freeze GMI vintages may adjudicate the claim. Do not reconstruct historical theme membership after observing winners.

### Kill interpretation

A failure closes **generalization to dynamic themes**, not an independently confirmed static-industry result.

---

## Program-level falsification

The broader durable-leadership program should be sharply narrowed or ended if:

1. DL-1 fails on untouched data after honest PIT classification and strong own-stock/common-factor controls; and
2. the only remaining support requires individually tailored options, flow, revision, theme, or network definitions plus multiple new licensed datasets.

At that point the rational ruling is **KILL / NO FURTHER INVESTMENT**, not serial feature-family search until something passes.
