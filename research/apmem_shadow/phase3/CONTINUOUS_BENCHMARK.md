# Persistent-opportunity benchmark - frozen before phase3 payoff inspection

The native replay and stock volatility fits have completed, but no phase3 market-outcome comparison has been inspected. This specifies the continuous benchmark already reserved by PROTOCOL.md; it is not a reaction to a favorable or unfavorable candidate result. All arms are descriptive controls outside the original 34 primary contrasts.

The question is whether a rare entry trigger is the right controller for a persistent opportunity. A trend can remain investable after the first T1/T2 onset. A system that holds for only 21 sessions after rare entries may have low return mainly because it rarely deploys capital. Conversely, continuous exposure can earn market beta without demonstrating stock-selection alpha. Both mechanisms must be visible.

Use the identical admitted 60-name primary panel, point-in-time membership filter, origin liquidity floor, 2020-01-02 through 2026-06-02 account window, 5bp/20bp cost cases, 80% maximum decision-open gross and ten slots. Only prior-close information selects the next-open target. Eligibility requires positive 63- and 126-session total-return momentum. Up to ten candidates receive 8% target equity each; unused slots stay in cash and weights are never renormalized upward. No leverage, shorting or prospective claim.

Six fixed arms:
1. Monthly (every 21 exchange sessions from the fixed start) constituent selection by stable identifier; no risk scaling.
2. Monthly constituent selection by descending positive 126-minus-21 relative momentum; no risk scaling.
3. Same monthly relative-momentum constituents; daily MEM risk target adjustment, capped at the original 8% per-name target.
4. Same as 3 with EWMA .94 instead of MEM.
5. Same monthly relative-momentum selection and MEM risk adjustment only on the monthly decision dates; intervening weights drift subject to the shared gross cap.
6. Daily relative-momentum constituent selection; no risk scaling, to expose the turnover/decay tradeoff.

At each required rebalance, solve self-financing transaction costs against post-cost account wealth; sell reductions before buying increases and never use unspendable distribution receivables. On non-rebalance days only known corporate actions, common gross-cap enforcement and terminal liquidation occur. Payment-date limitation and no-split sensitivity remain the same as ACCOUNTING_AND_COMPARISONS.md. Stock-specific target volatility is 30% as already frozen. Missing risk forecasts cannot create a zero-volatility or full-risk position.

Also print ONE fixed-initial-capital combination: 50% of starting wealth assigned to arm 3 and 50% to the registered T1/T2 union-MEM event policy, with separate cash accounts, no inter-sleeve transfers and no subsequent 50/50 rebalancing. Its combined wealth is the sum of the two independent account equities weighted by initial capital. This is an illustrative two-sleeve architecture, not optimized weights; it can hold up to twenty distinct names and is therefore not presented as identical to the ten-slot primary account. Existing per-sleeve costs are retained, no netting benefit invented, and overlapping names/aggregate exposure are reported where available. The fixed combination is evaluated on return, loss and exposure, not granted live allocation authority.

The goal is to isolate persistent opportunity, tactical entry and risk scaling. A higher-CAGR persistent arm does not by itself prove better entry timing; a lower-drawdown mixed book does not prove diversification if it simply holds more cash. Compare matching dates, capital usage, costs, adverse tails and foregone upside, and retain negative findings.
