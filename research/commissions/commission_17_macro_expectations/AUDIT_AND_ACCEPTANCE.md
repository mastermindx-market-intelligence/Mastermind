## Why the resulting plan is sounder

The revised first wave begins at existing owners and a concrete user capability: a release can be reconstructed and correctly explained through the actual producer/consumer path. It does not start by designing a generic store or acquiring every possible feed.

The economic layer now asks whether each quantity is identified. A survey median, a cross-section of forecast points, a subjective probability distribution, a futures-implied mean and a pricing density cannot quietly trade places. This prevents plausible-looking but invalid arithmetic and narratives.

The temporal layer makes testability explicit. “Publicly knowable” is a different claim from “our system actually held it.” Both differ from a later reconstruction. Later corrections and model fits cannot silently rewrite historical decisions.

The evaluation layer distinguishes explaining an announcement response from forecasting returns after a feasible decision time and forecasting future economic outcomes. It therefore avoids claiming prediction from an outcome already used in the inputs.

Source uncertainty remains a planned gate. A masterplan can be build-ready while a particular source remains unqualified; it must not call the source capability complete until the sample, rights and delivery evidence exist.

## Synthetic arithmetic verification

The masterplan's policy example assumes 30 calendar days, 20 at 4.33%, 10 post-effective days, and a futures index of 95.72:

- Monthly mean = 100 − 95.72 = 4.28%.
- Post-effective mean = [30 × 4.28 − 20 × 4.33] ÷ 10 = 4.18%.
- A: 0.4 × 0 bp + 0.6 × (−25 bp) = −15 bp; variance = 150 bp²; standard deviation = 12.2474487 bp.
- B: 0.7 × 0 bp + 0.3 × (−50 bp) = −15 bp; variance = 525 bp²; standard deviation = 22.9128785 bp.

The means agree while the probability of any cut differs. This is a proof of non-identification from a mean, not a forecast or actual historical FedWatch observation.

The payroll example separately preserves +40 thousand of current-period consensus error and −30 thousand of prior-period revisions. Adding them mechanically into a universal surprise score is explicitly rejected.
