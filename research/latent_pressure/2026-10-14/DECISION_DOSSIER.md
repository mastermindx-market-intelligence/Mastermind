## 1. Decision and scientific synthesis

**Do not promote the tested daily latent-pressure proxies.** They add no demonstrated incremental forecast benefit on the frozen ETF experiment. No new latent-accumulation score, dealer-inventory claim, forecast weight, automatic alert, rank or sizing input is authorized. Preserve the incumbent early-confirmation doctrine and existing display-only verdicts. The highest-value next experiment introduces **genuinely observed trade/quote information**, rather than another transformation of daily closes.

Public observations can measure liquidity, execution pressure, relative performance and participation. They do not uniquely recover institutional inventories or intentions. Every trade has a buyer and seller; buyer initiation describes crossing liquidity, not literal net capital entering a security. A relative return lead between themes does not prove the same investors transferred capital between them. Use observed pressure/inferred sponsorship language with unknown states and counterevidence.

### Front A: price/volume and institutional footprints

Wyckoff/VSA, volume/market profiles, anchored VWAP, point-of-control migration, compression and failed breakouts are candidate feature vocabularies, not a unified validated inventory detector. Define anchors and levels from trailing data, never the future lowest low. Test displacement/volatility/volume, predeclared level defense and time-to-failure. Executed-volume profiles do not reveal unexecuted demand or investor cost basis. Lo/Mamaysky/Wang's historical algorithmic pattern work supports testing conditional distributions, not accepting every discretionary chart. The selected pilot files lack intraday high/low/open, so the pilot does not test profile or detailed spread-analysis claims. [S1]

### Front B: microstructure and hidden liquidity

Cont/Kukanov/Stoikov's 50-stock TAQ study supports contemporaneous book-event imbalance/depth as a better explanation of short-interval price changes than volume alone. It does not establish a next-week breakout signal. Lillo/Mike/Farmer and Sato/Kanazawa support order-splitting/sign-persistence mechanisms; persistent signs need not imply predictable returns. Participant-level mechanism tests cannot be reproduced by pretending anonymous public prints identify institutions. [S2-S4]

Tsaknaki/Lillo/Mazzarisi's open paper studies MSFT March2020 and TSLA December2021 with trade-count intervals approximately1/3minutes. Online regimes help order-flow/impact prediction in that sample; the identification of inferred regimes with actual institutional metaorders remains conjectural. That is not multi-year cross-sector swing-alpha proof. [S5]

The candidate is **impact surprise conditional on signed flow, depth, spread, clock and volatility**, followed by an entirely later outcome. Aggressive buying resisted by replenishing asks may mean passive selling, not bullish accumulation; aggressive selling resisted by replenishing bids may still continue until the bids fail. Keep continuation/reversal/exhaustion outcomes separate. FINRA ATS aggregates and short-volume do not identify the passive counterparty. [S6-S7]

### Front C: options

Pan/Poteshman's important information set includes buyer-initiated opening transactions, unlike unsigned public OPRA volume. Ge/Lin/Pearson and Johnson/So motivate separating signed opening demand, aggregate activity and volatility-related information. Preserve quote age, moneyness, tenor, spread, earnings/corporate actions and multileg ambiguity; premium and delta weights do not magically reveal intent. Call volume can be hedging/spreads, puts can protect stock holdings, and OI changes do not identify the dealer book. [S8-S10]

GEX/vanna/charm are conditional scenario calculations when inventory is unknown. Publish sensitivity under plausible long/short/mixed dealer assumptions, not unique measured exposure. Cboe Open-Close offers richer exchange labels, but coverage, delivery lags and historical conventions require exact qualification. [S11]

### Front D: breadth and leadership

Equal-weight/cap-weight divergence, residual member-return distributions, new highs/lows and rank migration can describe internals. The existing raw broadening/composite forecast result is negative. A genuinely new test is dated **factor-residual breadth plus event-response asymmetry**, controlling common factors and surprise magnitude. Avoid counting the same name repeatedly across themes. Compare lead at the **same false-alarm budget**, not by celebrating more early alerts. Current classification/history limits prevent a clean long-history acceptance claim.

### Front E: economic and thematic migration

Cohen/Frazzini and newer geographic-link work support investigating delayed transmission across economic relationships, not assuming today's LLM theme graph forecasts capital movement. Separate source-backed supplier/customer/bottleneck edges, return-correlation edges and narrative co-mentions. Date edges by publication availability. Common shocks and overlapping members can manufacture propagation; use rewired/reversed-edge and factor controls across several unrelated themes, including failures. [S12-S13]

### Front F: macro and risk appetite

Rates, credit, dollar, commodities, volatility and style baskets condition pressure; they do not independently prove hidden selling. A strong cap-weight index with weak participation can end in correction, broadening or concentrated continuation. Use a small preregistered interaction set and original availability clocks. ALFRED supplies revision vintages; CFTC positioning is delayed. Do not double-count defensive RS, beta weakness, skew and breadth if they largely reflect the same risk factor. [S14-S15]

### Front G: fundamentals, ownership and events

Earnings/revisions, Form4,13F, corporate actions, share counts and supply-chain disclosures are contextual evidence with different delays. Use SEC acceptance/publication rather than fiscal period or transaction date. 13F ordinarily arrives up to45days after quarter end; Form4 generally within2businessdays, with exceptions. Neither establishes a live complete institutional book. Event-response patterns also require firm/event-selection controls; later counterevidence can change an inattention interpretation. ETF primary share changes differ from secondary turnover but still mix arbitrage, in-kind creation and discretionary allocation. [S16-S18]

### Front H: statistical models

Start with ridge logistic/linear baselines and proper scoring rules. Sequential tests need calibrated repeated-alarm thresholds. State-space models must filter, not smooth using future observations. HMM/HSMM can model state/duration without making those states actual inventories. BOCPD is suitable for online shifts, Hawkes models for event intensity, and survival/competing-risk models for breakout versus reversal with right-censoring. Each must earn incremental value. Deep order-flow results can have effective horizons of only a few price changes; no complexity-to-multiweek bridge is assumed. Published predictors can decay after discovery; literature is not a permanent trading certificate. [S5,S19-S22]
