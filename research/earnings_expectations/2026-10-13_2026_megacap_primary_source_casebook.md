## 2. First-order observed developments

- Alphabet's cloud growth **accelerated** from 48% to 63% to 82% across successive reported quarters (+15 then +19 percentage points); revenue growth rose from 18% to 22% to 24% YoY. This is operating acceleration, *not* evidence by itself that the stock was mispriced or that investors extrapolated the prior quarter.
- Amazon AWS growth accelerated 24% → 28% → 37% (+4 then +9 points). GAAP EPS in Q1 and Q2 is strongly affected by investment gains, while TTM cash outflow intensified. Positive cloud economics and sharply negative TTM FCF can coexist.
- Microsoft Azure and other cloud services growth accelerated 39% → 40% → 43% (+1 then +3 points), but the company cautions that investments and discrete gains affect GAAP/non-GAAP comparisons.
- Alphabet reported Q2 FCF of **-$5.855B** despite growth. The same release recorded $98.0B of net other-income gains largely arising from unrealized securities; comparing unadjusted GAAP EPS to investor fundamentals would be misleading.
- Amazon Q2 reported net income of $62.647B while recognizing **$53.4B pretax** non-operating other income primarily from Anthropic investments; net income is not an adequate proxy for recurring operating growth without adjustment.
- The three cases all represent AI-related cloud acceleration; they are not independent random samples of the market. Peer cross-influence and macro AI narratives make causal identification harder.

## 3. The seven unanswered market-expectation questions per event

For EACH event above, obtain and freeze the following before assigning a narrative label:

1. **Timing:** exact first source publication timestamp, SEC filing acceptance if different, timezone and actual session availability. File date is not sufficient for an after-close return formula.
2. **Street hurdle:** actual sell-side EPS/revenue/segment estimates and guidance basis as-known-at T-20, T-5, T-1. Match fiscal target, unit, currency and GAAP/adjusted accounting definitions; leave null rather than retrofitting.
3. **Previous management hurdle:** complete quantitative old guidance and relevant structural commitments on matching horizon, with condition/assumptions; never label this Street consensus.
4. **Price incorporation:** true split-adjusted prior close, T-20/T-10/T-5/T-1, first regular close after public news, H5/H20; sector/market/factor controls, realized volatility. Pre-event run-up is not observed until reconstructed.
5. **Forward reset:** analyst revisions *after* announcement, fixed-fiscal-year consensus changes, long-term growth and profit model updates, cut/raise breadth; ensure no label/horizon rollover masquerades as change.
6. **Capital economics:** normalize investment gains, one-time expenses, capex, change in FCF conversion and segment operating income. Distinguish spend supporting capacity from a proven incremental economic return.
7. **Competing explanations:** rates/factor shocks, contemporaneous macro/peer results, concentration/positioning, options implied move and public narrative. Don't assert institutional motive without observation.

## 4. Falsifiable contrasts to prioritize

**Contrast A: Alphabet April vs July.** Operating growth accelerated in both; ask whether the *incremental market surprise* differed because prior cloud expectations, FCF/capex burden, company guidance, model competition, or rate/positioning conditions shifted. Price reaction alone cannot answer which.

**Contrast B: Amazon February vs July.** AWS growth accelerated throughout. Test whether the sign and magnitude of pre-event expectations and capital-productivity forecasts changed; inspect how reported cash outflows were judged against the quality and durability of cloud growth.

**Contrast C: Microsoft January vs July.** Azure acceleration modestly improved in reported data. Quantify change in analyst outlook, cash-generation expectations, and the equity valuation hurdle; distinguish a depressed prior price from a large fundamental surprise.

**Contrast D: Peer read-through.** The three releases are temporally close in April and July. Predefine information transmission channels (shared enterprise AI demand, capex/compute supply chain, pricing) and measure whether peers moved *before* their own announcements. Remove sector factors. Exclude peer information published after each decision time.

**Contrast E: Disconfirm the central thesis.** If high-quality consensus vintages show investor hurdles consistently fell before July events despite prior operating acceleration, the straightforward “great Q1 makes a high-hurdle Q2” mechanism is wrong for that event. Report failure rather than redescribe it as hidden expectations without evidence.
